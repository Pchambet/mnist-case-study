MLP vs CNN on MNIST

What does convolution actually buy on handwritten digits, once accuracy is measured with paired statistics, calibration, robustness to shifted inputs, and a concrete automation rule?

1. Accuracy, with the noise measured

Both models are trained 3 times (different initialisation and batch order) on the same 54,000-image training split, early-stopped on a stratified 6,000-image validation split, and scored once on the untouched 10,000-image test set. The MLP reaches 97.71% ± 0.02%, the CNN 98.81% ± 0.11% (mean ± sd across seeds).

Within the MLP or the CNN, seeds move the error count by at most 23 digits; switching architecture moves it by 111.

Is the gap real or test-set luck? Two paired checks on the same 10,000 images. A paired bootstrap over test images puts the gain at +1.11 points (95% CI 0.90 to 1.32). McNemar's exact test, seed by seed, counts the digits only one model gets right: for seed 0, 44 digits only the MLP gets right versus 165 only the CNN gets right (63 both miss); the largest p-value over seeds is 7e-13.

Capacity or convolution? The CNN has about twice the MLP's parameters. A wider MLP (784-256-128-10, 235,146 parameters, more than the CNN) trained with the same recipe reaches 97.70% ± 0.28%, i.e. 230 errors: no better than the small MLP. The CNN beats it by +1.12 points (95% CI 0.92 to 1.32; largest McNemar p over seeds 4e-12), and the wide MLP is no more tolerant of shifts (70.9% at 2 px); under the error budget of section 3 it automates 75.0% of the stream. Parameter count does not explain the gap.

2. Robustness: move the digit a few pixels

MNIST digits are centred by centre of mass. Real inputs rarely are. Each test digit is shifted by d pixels in eight directions (zero-filled), and accuracy is averaged over directions.

At 2 px the MLP is at 71.0% while the CNN keeps 89.8%; at 4 px, 19.9% vs 42.4%. Neither model saw shifted digits in training. Convolution and pooling buy tolerance to one or two pixels, not invariance: past that, the CNN fails too. Training with random shifts (augmentation) is the standard fix and is not tested here.

3. The decision: how much can be automated?

Suppose digits are read automatically only when the model is confident, and the rest go to a human, with a budget of at most one error per 1,000 accepted digits (0.1%). The confidence threshold is chosen on the validation split, then frozen and applied to the test set.

Under the same budget the MLP automates 68.2% of the test stream (realised error 0.015%), the CNN 90.8% (realised error 0.059%): the manual queue shrinks from 31.8% to 9.3% of the stream, a far larger gap than the 1.11-point accuracy difference suggests. The thresholds are conservative: with hindsight (tuning on the test set itself, which a real deployment cannot do) the same budget would allow 82.4% and 93.4%. The 6,000-digit validation split simply contains few errors to calibrate a 0.1% threshold on, and the MLP pays more for that caution.

4. Can the confidences be trusted?

Expected calibration error (15 equal-width bins, mean over seeds): MLP 0.48%, CNN 0.24%. Points on the diagonal mean "90% confident" is right about 90% of the time. The diagram pools the 3 seeds; over 93% of digits fall in the top bin, which dominates the ECE. Good calibration lets the score be read as a probability; the threshold rule of section 3 only needs the ranking of confidences.

Limitations

  • 3 seeds per model is enough to show the seed spread is small next to the gap, not to estimate it precisely.
  • Neither architecture is tuned (no augmentation, no learning-rate schedule, no hyper-parameter search); the comparison is between two textbook models of the same order of size (the CNN has about twice the parameters), not between the best of each family.
  • Architecture and capacity are separated by one control only: the wide MLP matches the CNN's parameter count, but both MLPs use dropout and the CNN does not, and no regularisation was tuned for either.
  • The translation test is synthetic; it isolates one property (shift sensitivity) rather than modelling a real capture process. Diagonal directions move d px along each axis, and at 4 px about a quarter of shifted digits lose some stroke at the border (98.7% of ink kept on average), so part of the 4 px drop is lost information, not fragility.
  • The 0.1% budget threshold is set on 6,000 validation digits, i.e. about six tolerated errors, so it is itself noisy; the realised test error is reported next to it.
